# Why the protocol is shaped this way

Read only when the user or the stakeholder asks why the sessions work like this. Never recite it
during a session. Source: the author's essays at armandozaratem.com.

- **El lenguaje ubicuo no es un glosario.** A glossary lists words; a ubiquitous language is the
  agreement about how those words behave in cases. That is why the glossary stores edge cases,
  invariants and exclusions, and why questions ask for cases (Evans).
- **Sense and reference (Frege).** Two names can point to the same thing with different senses,
  and one name can point to two things. "Libro" at the lending desk (the copy) and in the catalogue
  (the title) is the second case — hence the `frontera-candidata` hotspot instead of a merged
  definition.
- **Language games and rule-following (Wittgenstein, §201).** No rule fixes its own application;
  every definition can be read two ways. Asking reduces ambiguity, it never exhausts it — which is
  why the closing criterion is "decided with an author or visibly open", never "eliminated".
- **Gavagai (Quine).** An answer is compatible with more than one meaning. Only a case that
  separates the readings settles it: the `repregunta`.
- **Programming as theory building (Naur).** The model lives in the people, not in the artifact.
  The AI therefore never completes it: what nobody adopted stays `hipótesis-sin-autor`.
- **Metaphors we live by (Lakoff and Johnson).** "Historial" or "foto" decides whether something is
  derived or stored. The metaphor question surfaces that before the code does.
- **Funes el memorioso (Borges).** To abstract is to forget. Every model forgets on purpose, so the
  forgetting is recorded as an exclusion with an author.
- **Los sinónimos son sospechosos. Pelea por los nombres.** Two words for one thing usually hide two
  things. The synonym detector opens a hotspot instead of merging.
